    - I think it is a very underrated thing to maintain a good relationship with your bank. I cant say how underrated it is. Smoother problem resolutions, Better negotiating powers. There are A LOT of benefits of this.
    - Now, how to even build a good relationship with your bank?
    - Keep one bank as your primary, you can and should have accounts in more than one banks. But keep one as primary with most of your cash inflow and outflow.
    - Maintain healthy balances, not just above minimum required balance. An account just sitting with some money is not a good sign of it being your GOTO bank.
    - Have multiple products with same bank (that is why I said good relations with good banks since you grow together in a way) like FDs, credit cards, maybe a demat account (if it is worth it... tbh for me zerodha is way more than enough, I have had a good amount of experience with trading so I could say – personal opinion – it is good and simple enough for most... yeah it has its problems and ofc you dont want your broker to have problems in middle of a trade lol... at least they had some back in 2022... but idk now... and even then it was way better than what most other brokers had to offer, some I know were even taken down my SEBI because of illegal forex stuff)
    - Pay everything on time and always
    - Avoid bouncing cheques (someone can sue you too if you are in India), closing and opening accounts frequently... ofc you will not look like a "good" customer
    - Just things similar to these, where both of you grow togther... yeah it does not mean you take bad deals just for the sake of having a good relationship with your bank.

    - **Credit Cards and a lot of yapping**

    - Now, another way is CIBIL score... and the best way to maintain it is by using taking credit. Yeah sounds hillarous for someone who is already struggling with it. But, I think this is the most doable for someone in my age group (again personal opinion... I am as noob as you... and you need to do your own research)
    - And having a good CIBIL score is not very hard if you are starting from scratch.
    - Now it is important to know that you dont take debt just for the sake of having a good number... NO! You need to understand the concept of good debt and bad debt first.
    - I dont wish anyone to have to live pay check to pay check because one of the main source of their expenses is debt payments.
    - A good debt is anything that grows you... like education loan, home loan etc. and a bad one is personal loan for consumption or the worst case, paying another debt off :skull:, car loans or anything tied to something which not necessarily grows you... this was a very simplistic definition. 
    - One of my most fav and accessible sources of debt is credit cards, if used properly. and **properly** does a lot of heavy lifting here :skull:
    - Credit cards is one of the most most most goated sources both in a good way and absolute worst ways. Like it has one of the highest interest rates usually around 3% per month (around 36% pa) when compared to other sources even in the absolute worst case it is no more than 10%... worst worst case (well not for risky loans like personal loans with no clear assets)
    - But before diving deeper into this. Having a credit card or making a good financial profile is not my goal at this age. It is building good habits.
    - Good habits around tracking, planning, budgeting, learning financial disipline, building a basic emergency fund. So, having a card with like 100s of offers and like lakhs in spending limits is an absolute shooting myself in my foot moment, both for me and the finserv providing me with it.
    - Before getting in all the credit card bs (actually not for my irl case... but I learnt it on the way), knowing those basics are really important. I should probably not think of loan or any financial goal in general if I spend like 30k+ INR each month on delivered food with a salary of 50 or even 200k as a single person with no responsibilies. So, basics are really crucial.
    - First I made a rough budget on usual months and I realised I spend like lets take some example, say 40k on commute and food (not realistic intentionally lol). Okay it sounds too ridiculous even as an example... say 4k, minus one 0 xd... then even in the worst case I wont spend over 7k a month... So, I need a card with about 25-30k limit. Since for a good credit profile I have to keep my usage limit under 30... a few percent up and down is fine
    - And what is more important than having 30% consumption is paying your bills timely before end of month or billing date.. else it would destroy both your "score" and account.
    - One more thing is that, which I realised even without owning a card is that... the notion of card makes it really really really easy for you to over spend on things.
    - Let's suppose you need to go out with your friends. You usually pay for yourself. But now, your card gives you points for every 150 rs spent. You decide to pay for everyone which you would have not done the either way. Or suppose your card gives you cashback on online orders. You would order more thinking it is cheaper... it is not!!
    - So, that is the discipline we need before spoiling ourselves.
    - Hence, at this age we do not need crazy cashbacks or insane offers. All we need is a card with just a functionality of buy now pay later and very less limits.
    - For someone at my age (again depends on your personal situation and goals... mine are ofc different thatn yours... there are many of those here which I haven't listed... so, dont follow blindly... it is just for awareness and for you to push... I am saying this again and again because this can be dangerous and stakes are real, I am only responsible for myself and you are only reponsible for yourself... you are an adult) and with my goals... A UPI focused card with a limit of 30k is way way more than enough.
    - But, there is a good chance you wont be eligible for the card... yes my dear, you wont be... lekin it is important to understand, the credit card is nothing more than a loan... So, you can take some credit against some collateral or asset. Like you can get an FD and take a very very basic card against it.
    - A UPI focused card works for someone like me who does not order stuff online, does not spend a lot... it is mostly autos, metro card recharges and food. Which I all pay in cash. So, yeah I dont need a fancy card with 100s of deals. I am giving my example for someone who is reading (ik no one) this and can learn how to think for themselves and become financially literate themselves.
